Armando Lee, Bill Parlee, Tom
Rood, and myself as chairman.
along with four other contributing
judges. Charles Anchor, Cla-
rence Culwell, Conrad Meinert
and Patrick Vance, shared their
thoughts on exhibiting pairs with
the entire Judges Panel. The opin-
ions expressed were well-
considered and valuable. providing
the Judges Panel an overview of
differing approaches to exhibiting
pairs. Among our contributors
there was a blend of experience on
the NFSS Panel - from just a few
years all the way up to NFSS' in-
ception (and beyond). a good mix
geographically, and a range of
viewpoints.

In September 2001 the 29 mem-
bers of the NFSS Judges Panel
were asked to select from 2 Pairs
Standard choiees which reflected
the major viewpoints presented -
one in which the pairs would be
required to be matched in all ways
(the final approved standard). or
one in which the pairs would be
matched but with one exception -
no matched erested pairs would be
exhibited. A third approach to
pairs exhibiting was considered -
the “breeding pairs" point of view.
As presented by 3 judges in our
discussions this past summer.
“breeding pairs" were defined as
(1) cock and hen, and (2) combina-
tions of birds which are "safe" to
breed. But after considering the
idea of exhibiting "breeding pairs".

we agreed that the list of "unsafe
breeding combinations" that we
would have to disqualify from the
judging bench would. as Sally said
simply. be too long and flawed.
The response from our Judges
Panel was strong indeed with 20
members of our Judges Panel sub-
miiting votes. of which slightly
over a majority, 12 votes, were for
completely matched pairs. The
voting results reflected remarkably
well the balance of opinions we've
heard over the years. The results of
the Committee's work and Judges
Panel vote was submitted to and
approved by the NFSS Board last
fall. and the NFSS Pairs Standard
became official.

From our work this sammer, NFSS
can feel confident that the resulting
proposed NFSS Pairs Standard will
be a powerful tool in the critieal
evaluation of our finches and soft-
bills - in our birdrooms, in the mar-
ketplace, or at the show. I am
grateful for the tremendous partici-
pation of our Panel Judges in this
discussion this past summer and
for the opportunity to work with
them. They are outstanding!
