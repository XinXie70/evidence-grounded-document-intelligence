Status

Prof. Payne found L. virata to
be the most common species in
the area in which he was con-
ducting studies, which was a
scrubby patch of about 40 square
miles. Because of the difficulty in
getting around in other places
one cannot say for sure how
many other sites were as good or
what the global population size is.
In the study area it is estimated
there were around 40 pairs per

square mile. and because com-
parable habitat is widespread
Prof. Payne believes there could
be at ieast 100,000 individuais of
this species in Mali. it is also sus~
pected that, based on satellite
photographs that show habitat
and topography, a comparably
large number may also be found
in eastern Senegal.

The species is not considered
to be endangered or threatened,
due to its occurring over a wide
area and being common within
that area. Around 100 individuais
were also spotted at two bird
dealers in Africa, and Clive Bar-
low had managed to acquire
about 50 from another dealer in
Senegal only 12 months earlier
and took them back to his home
in The Gambia. Both sites appar-
ently export a lot, but the birds
are mistabeled "L. rubricata". To
compound matters, Senegal ac-
