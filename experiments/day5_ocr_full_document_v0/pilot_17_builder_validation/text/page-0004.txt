NFSS President's Message, May, 2002

Everyone always seems to want their chil-
| dren to have it better than they had it. Can
: this also apply to NFSS?

The current energetic and devoted Board
of Directors seems determined that future
NFSS members have it better than past mem-

bers. Because of increased computer avail-
| ability, this seems to be happening, and in a
kinder, gentler, more immediate way.

NFSS members now have more readily available information from
the expanded web site and newly titled NFSS Journal. They have
more immediate access to expert resources on NFSS Ya-
hoogroups.com, for day-to-day avian related problems. There is
faster board-member-to-board-member communication via the NFSS

_ Officer's group use. Pay-pal offers a quicker way to receive member-
ship payments and FinchShop revenue, but has some lessons-to-
learn.

I like that all officers seem to provide, via the NFSS Yahoo
groups, on-going input on what they are aiming for, are accomplish-
ing, where their problems are, and when they need advice from the
board or others. I also like the creative energy that is re-thinking
the roles of each officer, elected or appointed, and asking us to give
voting rights to all of those individuals who shape and maintain the
NFSS. Further, adversaries to bird/animal breeding force us to
become more politically astute, active and “politically correct". In
response, I particularly like that heretofore quiet members have
stepped forward to lead us in ‘protective’ new goals and objectives.
In that direction we will be defining a political activist officer's job
this quarter.

As the weather changes to summer, we must shift our focus
from cold/coel weather management to both the pleasure AND the
perils summer heat brings to avian culture. Foods spoil sooner, in-
sects and preditors are on the prowl, nests are filled with chicks,
fledglings are everywhere, banding becomes serious focus, and rival-
ries for mates teaches us that even the docile can cause injury when
competition for the finest feather takes its primal course.

The summer opens us to easier travel to avian events, outdoor
bird marts, and, increased caution regarding quarantine, and being
on guard for pests and illnesses.

Keep the seed cups full!

Sally, tington,President
