Habitat, general behaviour and
feeding

it is found in rocky sandstone
areas with bushes, scrub and
grass, and in most habits it is
reported to be very similar to
other firefinches. particularly
Jameson's and the African Fire-
finch to which it is closely related.
Clive Barlow intormed me that it
feeds on grass seeds and most
likely small insects, especially
termites. My captive birds aiso
love to chew growing grass and
the leaves of plants.

Courtship display

The majority of times | ob-
served the courtship display it
started with the male flying up to
a perch with a piece of dried
grass or coconut fibre in his bill,
carried by one end. The male
would then stretch his body ai-
most upright and begin to bob up
and down. | never once heard
him sing during this display, after
which he would jump to the
ground and repeat the process
there under cover. The female, if
receptive, would squat down and
vibrate her tail. Copulation would
then take place. Less frequently, |
have also observed copulation.
which | have timed at taking 16-
20 seconds, take place on a
perch and, more recently, once
on the edge of a water dish. In
the main. however. no display at
all is given prior to copulation.
The male merely follows the fe-
male around uttering a regular
soft fu-fu-tu-tu-tu call. The fe-
male, after allowing a short
“chase”, generally squats down
and copulation immediately fol-
lows.
